PEOPLE EXPERIENCES IN M365 · iOS & ANDROID

A Richer Profile Card for Mobile

Bringing People Experiences in M365 to mobile while validating a compact, scalable, and shareable architecture.

Read the case study ＋

The opportunity

The mobile contact card offered less context than other People Experiences in M365. Time zones, organisation structure, and professional details were harder to reach, while the existing framework did not scale.

Discovery & scope

I mapped features across web and mobile, audited the existing card, and shaped a proof of concept. The roadmap prioritised valuable richer-profile capabilities while preserving familiar mobile features.

Decisions & trade-offs

I chose replacement over incremental updates to align with the React initiative, balancing migration risk against a stronger foundation. Scope was tailored to mobile rather than copying every web feature. Feature continuity remained a release priority.

Execution

I led discovery, scope, roadmap, and alignment with the mobile partner team. Engineering built the React Native proof of concept; internal testing and bug bashes informed fixes before a phased worldwide release.

Outcome

The richer profile card shipped to production worldwide on iOS and Android.

What I’d do differently

I would advocate earlier with senior stakeholders and define success measures before testing. Proposed future criteria include 85% unassisted core-task success and a 10% relative lift in meaningful contact actions; these are retrospective targets, not measured results.

PEOPLE EXPERIENCES IN M365 · WEB & MOBILE

Reachability

Helping hybrid workers see the best time and way to reach a colleague, right from their profile card.

Read the case study ＋

The opportunity

Presence, calendar, working hours, time zone, and work location were scattered. Hybrid workers had to piece together whether it was a good time to contact someone.

What I shipped

A panel that brings presence and next availability, working hours and local time, and today’s work location into one place. In People Experiences in M365 meetings, the profile card also makes this context accessible from participant names.

Validating the launch

I partnered with data science on a two-week, hypothesis-driven A/B test. Its results secured the go decision for general availability.

Outcome

The panel now appears in 93% of people experiences sessions. After launch, I tracked adoption and engagement through telemetry, reported progress to leadership, and contributed to end-user documentation.

Launch & evangelism

The work featured in Microsoft’s May 2024 Microsoft Places launch.

INDEPENDENT PRODUCT CASE STUDY · CONCEPT, NOT LAUNCHED

Pickup Confidence

Helping Uber riders act before a pickup goes wrong.

By Melinda Kahi · Product research, persona, journey mapping & concept design

Uber connects riders with nearby drivers in a few taps. After a match, the rider sees the driver, the car, and an estimated pickup time. Most of the time that’s enough. When the estimate starts to slip, though, the rider is left watching a car icon and guessing whether to keep waiting.

I reviewed public app-store feedback, third-party consumer reviews, and Uber’s public help and reservation information. From recurring themes, I built a proto-persona and a journey map, then designed a concept for the moment when a pickup becomes less reliable.

The Time-Critical Rider

A proto-persona is a fictional profile built from real signals. Here, those signals come from public feedback rather than interviews. Amani is 29, based in Nairobi, and rides to client meetings, early flights, and evenings out. She doesn’t mind a short wait. What she minds is not knowing whether to keep waiting.

Map for the journey

Amani sets her pickup point, checks the price, and confirms. She’s matched with a driver four minutes away and relaxes. Then the estimate grows from 4 to 9 to 12 minutes while the car barely moves. Eventually the driver cancels, or the app starts searching again. She has to rebook, switch apps, or contact support about a charge.

Frustrated Amani

Four themes in public feedback shaped the problem framing:

  • Waiting for a matched driver who then cancels.
  • Changing pickup times or rematching with no explanation or control.
  • A scheduled ride feeling like a promise, even though public terms don’t guarantee pickup.
  • Worry about extra costs, missed plans, and a weak recovery path after a failed pickup.

The pressing issue

The app tells Amani what’s happening, but not what to do about it. By the time a pickup clearly fails, she has lost the time she needed to make another plan. The moment that matters comes earlier, while she can still choose.

This is directional research from public feedback, not a representative study. It does not establish prevalence or causes. Rider interviews are needed to validate the problem before building.

A clear signal

I’d give each matched pickup a simple, honest state that changes as the signals change. Explain delays, offer clear alternatives while the rider can still act, and show fee status immediately after a cancellation.

01 · On track

02 · Delayed

03 · At risk

04 · Driver cancelled

Unbranded concept screens, not Uber’s interface. Names, plates, times, and prices are examples.

Expected behaviour

Each state needs a clear trigger. Thresholds are placeholders to calibrate against real pickup baselines. No-fee cancellation is a policy hypothesis to test with marketplace operations, not a promise.

Trade-offs

  • Proactive recovery: early warnings and choices before a long wait, rather than support only after failure.
  • Honest expectations: explain what changed without implying a match or reservation guarantees pickup.
  • Rider protection and marketplace cost: validate fee relief as a policy hypothesis.
  • Focused scope: no full app redesign, driver-app changes, pricing-model changes, or support rebuild.

How I’d measure it

There is no baseline yet and the concept is untested. One proposed concept-test criterion is that at least 80% of participants can correctly explain the pickup state and why it changed. These are proposed validation criteria, not product results.
